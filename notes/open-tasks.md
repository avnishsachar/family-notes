## Facts from the 2 Oct summary that change the plan

- Essential hypertension is a listed diagnosis, and BP was 250/160 at the collapse. The 2 Oct medication list has no blood pressure tablet. She needs one unless the second discharge added it.
- Atorvastatin 40 mg at night was prescribed. Statins commonly cause symmetrical calf and thigh aching, worse at night. This is a candidate cause of the calf pain.
- Aspirin 100 mg daily was prescribed on the neurointerventional team's advice. An ICU note mentions a stent. Clarify whether a stent was placed, because that sets how long aspirin continues and affects any NSAID use.
- Melatonin 6 mg at night and lactulose were already prescribed.
- Sodium, potassium, magnesium and phosphate were all low at some point in the admission.
- OT noted left-sided visual inattention on 30 Sep, improving with prompting.
- 28 Sep CT reported a left frontal low-density area; the 4 Oct CT and MRI place it on the right, along the right frontal drain track, and call it a small encephalomalacic focus (a scar). Unchanged across scans. The 28 Sep laterality was almost certainly a typo.
- 4 Oct MRI also reports "significant involutional changes" (volume loss) and periventricular white-matter change. Ask neuroradiology what the volume loss is compared against, and whether the periventricular change is fluid seepage from the hydrocephalus (which the shunt treats) or small-vessel disease from blood pressure.
- Basal ganglia calcification on both 4 Oct scans: common incidental finding, usually of no consequence.
- Shortened APTT on 4 Oct (24.4 s). Minor, but a mild pro-clotting signal worth mentioning when asking for the leg Doppler.
- No air travel for at least 1 month from 2 Oct, pending clearance.
- Follow-up arranged: neurosurgery 1 week, neurology 1 week, psychiatry 2 weeks, neurointerventional imaging as advised.

## Call the same day if

- Readings persistently at or above 160/100 despite rest and paracetamol.
- One calf swollen, hot, red or tender compared with the other.
- Fever 37.5 or above.
- Confusion clearly worse than the day before.
- Headache that depends on position (worse upright, better flat = over-drainage; worse flat or on waking = under-drainage).

## Emergency department, do not wait

- Breathlessness, chest pain, oxygen below 94, coughing blood, fainting.
- Drowsy or hard to wake.
- Fever with headache or vomiting.
- Sudden severe headache, new weakness, trouble speaking or seeing.
- Redness, swelling or leaking along the shunt tubing or wounds.
